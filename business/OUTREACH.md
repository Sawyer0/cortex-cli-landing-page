## Executive Summary

NYC hospitals under Article 28 of the Public Health Law face immediate and expanding cybersecurity reporting obligations:

- **Effective Oct 2, 2024:** 72-hour mandatory notification to NYSDOH of material cybersecurity incidents
- **Effective Oct 2, 2025:** Full compliance with comprehensive cybersecurity program requirements, including designated CISO, annual risk assessments, MFA, penetration testing, and incident response planning

**Key insight for My pilot:** The 72-hour reporting requirement creates immediate operational friction. A **Zero-Trust Compliance Agent** that automates incident classification using **Netflow metadata (no PHI access)** and produces **cryptographically verifiable audit trails** directly reduces regulatory risk. The target buyer is the CISO/GRC function requiring mathematical assurance of compliance, not just operational speed.

## III. Regulatory Context: NYSDOH 10 NYCRR 405.46

### Current Requirements (Effective Oct 2, 2024)

- **Immediate reporting obligation:** Notify NYSDOH within 72 hours of determining a material cybersecurity incident
- **Definition:** Material incident = incident with materially adverse impact on operations, reasonable likelihood of material harm, or ransomware deployment in material part of systems
- **Documentation retention:** 6 years of incident-related records must be maintained

### Full Compliance Requirements (Effective Oct 2, 2025)

- Designate a CISO (internal or external)
- Conduct annual cybersecurity risk assessments
- Establish comprehensive written cybersecurity program
- Implement MFA for external-facing systems
- Conduct annual penetration testing and vulnerability scans
- Provide mandatory cybersecurity training for all staff
- Establish incident response and recovery protocols
- Maintain continuous network monitoring with logging and detection

### Why This Matters for My Pilot

**The bottleneck:** Hospitals must determine whether an incident is "material" within 72 hours and produce documentation. Right now, this decision-making process is manual and distributed across incident response teams, IT leadership, compliance, and legal.

**My value proposition:** A tool that standardizes incident classification (**mathematically proving "Materiality" decisions via tamper-evident audit logs**), auto-generates evidence/documentation, and tracks 72-hour SLA timers reduces:

- Time to determination (currently highly variable)
- Draft document completeness (reducing back-and-forth with NYSDOH)
- Regulatory risk (clear audit trail and standardized workflow)
- CISO/GRC team stress (automated compliance-ready package)
